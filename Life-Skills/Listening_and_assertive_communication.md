# Listening and Assertive Communication

## Question 1: What are the steps or strategies for active listening?


1. Avoid distractions and focus on the conversation, don't get zoned-out while you are     in conversation. Example : thinking about a movie or future goals in between                    conversation.
2. Do not interrupt while the other person is speaking.
3. Let the speaker finish their thoughts before responding.
4. Use appropriate body language to show that you are listening.
5. Use encouraging phrases or "door openers" to encourage the person to continue
   speaking. example: that sounds interesting, how can i do that? .
6. Take notes during important conversations or meetings.

These strategies help ensure that we fully understand the speaker instead of simply hearing their words.

## Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of reflective listening?

According to Fisher's model, reflective listening involves the following:

1. Remove distractions and give your full attention to the speaker.
2. Try to see the situation from their point of view without judging or necessarily         agreeing with them.
3. Make the speaker feel understood and comfortable sharing their thoughts.
4. Notice their feelings and respond with appropriate words and body language.

5. Take notes when necessary
6. Explain what you understood in your own words.Give the speaker an opportunity to           correct you if you have misunderstood something.


## Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some obstacles I face in my listening process are:

1. I get zoned-out if conversation is not that important.
2. I start thinking about my response before the other person has finished speaking.
3. I assume that I already understand what the speaker means instead of listening carefully because, I have read once about it.Even if I am not 100% sure.


## Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

To improve my listening, I can:

1. Give my complete attention to the speaker.
2. Avoid preparing my response while the other person is still speaking.
3. Keep an open mind instead of being over-confident even if I have read about topic.
   Share my understanding with the speaker and ask them to correct me if I have             misunderstood something.

## Question 5: When do you switch to a passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I sometimes become passive when I disagree with someone but, do not want to express my opinion because I want to avoid conflict. For example, I may agree with a plan suggested by a group even when I have a different idea.

## Question 6: When do you switch to an aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may become aggressive when I feel frustrated, repeatedly misunderstood, or when the same problem happens many times. In such situations, I may speak too quickly, use a louder tone, or focus more on proving that I am right than on understanding the other person's point of view.

## Question 7: When do you switch to a passive-aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?

I may become passive-aggressive when I am upset but do not directly communicate the reason. For example, instead of clearly saying that something has bothered me, I might become quiet, give short answers, use sarcasm, or make indirect comments. This can happen when I want to avoid direct confrontation but, still feel frustrated.

## Question 8: How can you make your communication more assertive?

I can make my communication more assertive by following these steps:

1. Express my thoughts clearly and directly instead of expecting others to guess what I   mean.
2. Use a calm and respectful tone and body language, even when I disagree with someone.
3. Listen to the other person's point of view before responding.
4. Say no when necessary without being rude or feeling guilty.Don't take mean comments,     like its fine, point-out that instead.
5. Control my emotions before responding, especially when I am angry or frustrated.
   Else, it will lead to wrong decisions.
