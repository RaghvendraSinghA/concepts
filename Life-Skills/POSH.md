# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (PoSH)

## 1. What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment can be physical, verbal, or non-verbal, and includes both a single incident and repeated behaviour.     

Some examples are:   

- **Physical conduct**: unwelcome touching, brushing against someone, physical assault, or any unwanted physical contact like hugs.    
- **Verbal conduct**: sexually remarks or jokes, comments on someone's body or appearance, repeated unwelcome    
   requests for dates or favours.
- **Non-verbal conduct**: showing sexually explicit materials like wall-posters or sending jokes with explicit images or computer wallpaper.    
- **Creating a hostile work environment**: such as persistent inappropriate jokes, comments, or a general atmosphere of sexually
  charged behaviour that interferes with someone's ability to work comfortably.
- **Power-based harassment**: a senior colleague, manager, or someone in a position of authority using that position to pressure
  or offering favourable treatment (promotions, good assignments) in exchange for sexual favours.



## 2. What would you do in case you face or witness any incident or repeated incidents of such behaviour?

- **Say no / set a boundary**: if safe to do so, clearly tell the person the behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop.
- **Document everything**: note down dates, times, locations, what happened, and any witnesses. Save any relevant messages,
   emails, or screenshots as evidence.
- **Don't stay silent**: whether you're the person affected or a witness, report the incident,   
  staying silent or ignoring it can allow the behaviour to continue or escalate.
- **Report it through the proper channel**: report it to your manager, HR, higher authority or Internal committee.     
- **Support the person affected (if you're a witness)**: offer to be a witness in the inquiry.
- **Escalate if needed**: if the internal process is mishandled or ignored, take legal action under the PoSH Act.
