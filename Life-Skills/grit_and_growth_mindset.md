# Grit and Growth Mindset

## Question 1: What is Grit?

Grit is the ability to stay committed to a long-term goal and continue working despite failures, difficulties, or slow progress. Talent is useful, but consistent effort and perseverance are often more important for achieving success.

## Question 2: Introduction to Growth Mindset

A **growth mindset** is the belief that our abilities and intelligence can improve through learning, practice, effort, and feedback. Instead of seeing failure as proof that we are incapable, we can view it as an opportunity to learn and improve.

## Question 3: What is the Internal Locus of Control? What is the key point in the video?

**Internal Locus of Control** means believing that our actions, decisions, and efforts have a significant influence on the results we achieve in life.

The key point is that instead of focusing too much on things we cannot control, we should focus our energy on the things we **can control**. For example, we may not be able to control the difficulty of an assignment, the outcome of an interview, or other people's opinions, but we can control our preparation, effort, practice, attitude, and response to difficulties.

## Question 4: What are the key points mentioned by the speaker to build a Growth Mindset?

* Believe that abilities and intelligence can be developed.
* See challenges as opportunities to learn and grow.
* Focus on learning instead of trying to appear smart.
* Treat mistakes and failures as feedback.
* Replace negative thoughts with more constructive ones.
* Accept that improvement takes time and consistent effort.
* Learn from criticism and feedback.
* Focus on the learning process, not only the final result.
* Keep practicing difficult skills.
* Learn from the success and methods of other people.
* Step outside your comfort zone.
* Be patient with your own progress.

## Question 5: What are your ideas for taking action and building a Growth Mindset?

I can take the following actions to build a growth mindset:

* I will focus on improving my skills instead of worrying about whether I am naturally talented.
* When I do not understand a programming concept, I will not say, **"I cannot do this."** Instead, I will say, **"I do not understand this yet."**
* I will break difficult problems into smaller parts and solve them step by step.
* I will practice coding concepts regularly instead of only watching tutorials.
* I will treat bugs and mistakes as opportunities to understand how something works.
* I will ask questions and seek feedback whenever I get stuck.
* I will review my mistakes and try to understand why they happened.
* I will avoid comparing my progress too much with others and focus on my own improvement.
* I will take responsibility for the things I can control, such as my effort, practice, preparation, and attitude.
* I will stay consistent even when progress feels slow.
* I will challenge myself by working on problems that are slightly outside my current comfort zone.
* I will remember that becoming a good software developer is a continuous learning process, and I do not need to know everything immediately.
